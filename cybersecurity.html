A Nation Under Digital Transformation

Qatar's National Vision 2030 has accelerated digital adoption across government, energy, finance, and healthcare. With that transformation comes heightened cyber risk — and a correspondingly rigorous regulatory environment designed to protect national interests, citizen data, and critical infrastructure from both domestic and foreign threats.

Organizations operating in Qatar are subject to some of the most comprehensive cybersecurity mandates in the Gulf region. Non-compliance is not merely a reputational risk — it carries legal penalties, operational restrictions, and potential exclusion from government contracts.

01

National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA)

The primary regulator overseeing national cybersecurity policy, incident response, and mandatory compliance frameworks

02

National Information (NIA) Framework

•A mandatory set of controls and certification requirements for organizations handling sensitive government or national-security data.

03

Qatar Cybersecurity Framework (QCSF)

•A sector-specific framework establishing baseline security requirements across regulated industries.

04

Personal Data Privacy Protection (Law No. 13 of 2016)

Qatar's foundational data privacy legislation governing the collection, processing, and storage of personal data.

05

National Data Classification Policy 3.0

The governing standard for classifying, handling, and protecting data based on sensitivity and national-security impact.

Service Line 2

Critical Infrastructure Protection

Qatar's energy, utilities, and industrial sectors are among the most strategically important — and most targeted — assets in the region. Our Critical Infrastructure Protection services are specifically designed for organizations operating Operational Technology (OT) and Industrial Control Systems (ICS) in high-consequence environments.

Critical Infrastructure · Deep Dive

OT/ICS Security Assessments

  • Asset Inventory & Network Mapping — Complete identification of all OT/ICS assets, communication protocols, and network segmentation — often revealing undocumented systems that represent unknown risk.
  • Vulnerability Assessment — Specialized scanning and manual testing of OT environments using methodologies that avoid disruption to live industrial processes.
  • Threat Modeling — Analysis of adversary tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) specific to the energy and utilities sector, including nation-state threat actors.
  • Segmentation & Architecture Review — Evaluation of IT/OT network boundaries, DMZ configurations, and data diode implementations against IEC 62351 and NIST SP 800-82 standards.
A successful cyberattack on OT/ICS infrastructure can result in physical damage, environmental harm,
production shutdown, and national-security consequences — far beyond traditional data breach impacts.
